Reinterpretation as an Operation
The gap The ladder Prior work The operation Results The boundary Data

2,327 prizes  /  14 systems on one ladder  /  one missing operation

A breakthrough in physics is rarely a better fit to the data nowadays.

More often it is a decision that the data was about something else. Lorentz and Einstein predicted the same numbers; Einstein’s theory dropped an aether velocity no measurement could reach. Automated discovery has no operation for that move. This project measures how big the gap is, builds an operation that performs the move, and then bounds what it cannot reach.

The gap

65% of awarded physics work changed a description — against 45% across science as a whole. In physics, reframing is not a side issue. It is the main road.

Machine learning has an operation for one rung of six.

We annotated 2,327 scientific prizes by what the contribution did rather than by how it was cited. Restricting to the ten physics prizes gives 532 events, and the contrast with science at large is why this work is about physics.

L0 is not the shallow end — a Fields Medal can sit there. It is simply the only rung for which machine learning has an operation.

L0 Regression
Keep the description you have and solve inside it. Dirac Medal 2025 — Kerr. An exact rotating black-hole solution of Einstein’s equations. Nothing about general relativity changed; he solved a hard problem in the language it already had. The only rung machine learning has an operation for — and not the easy one.
L1 Reinterpretation
The theory predicts the same numbers but carries less. Heineman Prize 1983 — Kruskal. The surface at r = 2M in the Schwarzschild solution was taken for a physical singularity, a place where spacetime breaks down. Kruskal’s coordinates showed there is nothing there: it is an artifact of the chart, not of the spacetime. Identical predictions, and a supposed feature of the world deleted — the same shape as Einstein dropping the aether. 38 events — the rarest move in the corpus.
L2 Retyping
Decide two things are the same, or that one thing is really two. Dirac Medal 2024 — Casini, Huerta, Ryu & Takayanagi. Stop measuring raw entanglement entropy, which diverges, and measure relative entropy instead. Same physics, a different quantity counted as the thing. Most description-changing work is here.
L3 Transfer
Carry one field’s language into another. Dirac Medal 2019 — Mukhanov, Starobinsky & Sunyaev. The citation says it outright — “combining microscopic physics with the large scale structure of the universe.”
L4 New object
Add a kind of thing the language did not have. Dirac Medal 2020 — Neveu, Ramond & Virasoro. Cited for work “which introduced new Bosonic and Fermionic symmetries into physics.” The prize text names the move.
L5 New move
Change what counts as a move at all. Cohen 1966 — forcing. Not a physics award; none reaches this rung. A general method for building models to order — a new kind of move rather than a new result. One candidate in 1,547 events, and the annotator flagged even that one uncertain.

Nothing performs L1. No system takes a theory that already fits and returns a theory that fits identically while carrying less — the Lorentz-to-Einstein move. And a language model does not close it: on five episodes it scored 87% when it could recognise them and 48%, chance, when the names were stripped out. It recalls; it does not judge.

The operation

Structure no measurement can see — and why you must not simply delete it.

“Carries structure no measurement can see” is not a metaphor. For a theory predicting f(x,p), the directions along which no prediction moves are the null space of the prediction Jacobian. Removing them is reinterpretation.

Measured against the error bars, not exactly: Lorentz’s aether was not unobservable in principle, only to the precision of the day.

Applied naively, this deletes conservation laws. A global phase rotation is invisible to every probe exactly as a gauge transformation is. Quotient both and you do not discover charge conservation — you erase the conserved quantity.
invariant applied point by point redundancy → quotient it L1
invariant only applied uniformly symmetry → keep it, read off a conserved charge L4

One test, two discovery operations. The second is the more productive: a distinction no probe can see, but only when moved everywhere at once, is a conservation law. Our implementation refuses to quotient at all when no locality test is available, rather than guess.

Results

It finds symmetries nobody named, and refuses to delete them.

On a periodic 6×6 lattice with a two-flavour matter field and U(1) links, the mechanism is handed eight candidate generators and told nothing about which are real invariances.

The proposer

The model proposes in both worlds. Only the measurement tells them apart.

A null-space search finds only what its basis contains, so something must widen it. We use the language model only to generate, never to decide.

worldresidualproposedaccepted
Aa real missing concept, 0.4·sin(3x)82–3
Bcontrol — pure noise, nothing to find80

Offered the truth directly the filter is decisive — sin(3x) reaches 97% coverage against 68% for sin(2.9x) and 20% for wrong terms. So the proposer is the bottleneck, not the test: across runs the model offered sin(x), sin(2x), x³ and sin(πx) — never sin(3x), though the residual is a clean sine.

Where it goes quiet

On a real controversy, the criterion is silent — and that is the result.

NGC 3198 from SPARC, fitted by a dark-matter halo and by MOND. No ground truth; that is what makes it free of the leakage that makes historical cases worthless for testing a model that has read the histories.

descriptionconceptsχ²/Nunobservable

Neither carries structure the measurements cannot see: the halo model’s softest direction still moves the curve 6.4σ — unsurprising, since SPARC’s 3.6 µm photometry was assembled to break exactly that degeneracy. The Lorentz–Einstein signature needs two descriptions that agree on the data. These disagree at 1.22 against 9.21, and where descriptions disagree ordinary model comparison settles it. The operation applies to a narrower class of disputes than we assumed.

The boundary

Four of eight Standard Model concepts were bought by a theorem, before any experiment.

The mechanism buys concepts with measurements. Against eight concept-forming episodes in the Standard Model it accounts for two: the neutrino, and gauge redundancy. The other six do not fail for want of cleverness. Four contain no probe at all.

  • Δ++ violated Fermi statistics. Colour was invented so the state would be allowed.
  • FCNC were too suppressed. Charm was invented so the excess would cancel.
  • Gauge anomalies had to sum to zero, which required complete generations.
  • WW scattering had to stay unitary, putting the Higgs below about a TeV — and telling experimenters what to build.
Every one predicted a real particle. Every one was paid for by a theorem first, with the experiment arriving years or decades later. A framework whose founding commitment is that a concept is paid for by an experiment has these exactly backwards.

The prerequisite is not more data or a better search: you can only have theorems if there is a formal theory to have them about, and no discovery system we know of carries one. That, rather than the difficulty of reinterpretation, is what bounds automated physics discovery today.

The weaker version of this route is already refuted here. Proposing concepts from structural defects in a description was tested on three substrates and lost on all three. The difference is grade, not kind: heuristics say a defect is a hint, theorems say a state is forbidden or it is not.

Read this before using any of it

One annotator, and it is a language model.

These are not the judgements of human domain experts. Every count here is one annotator’s reading of the official citation text, under human direction. There is exactly one annotator, so no inter-annotator agreement statistic exists — and we do not report a proxy for one.

The criterion was revised against known answers

An earlier form was modified eight times, each after seeing a verdict we knew to be wrong. That is why the results here rest on a lattice system with an independently known answer, and on a controversy with no known answer at all.

The acceptance rule does not discriminate

“Coverage strictly improves” admitted sin(x) and x³ at 20–22% against a base of 18%. It ranks correctly but needs a margin, not a strict inequality.

The scheme is not symmetric

The facts column distinguishes facts newly in hand from reach newly extended, but encodes that as two categories rather than as a ladder. A scheme that graded every column would be cleaner. We did not build one.

Hindsight and selection

Prizes are awarded with decades of hindsight, so the corpus samples work that turned out to matter, and citations justify a decision already taken.

What would help most

Re-annotate a sample. Disagree with us in public.

We cannot tell from the inside whether this scheme is reproducible or whether it records one system’s habits. Only someone else applying it can settle that.

  1. Sample a few hundred events from classifications.csv, spread across prizes and eras.
  2. Read only the citation text. That is all our annotator saw.
  3. Assign a mode, and a depth if a description changed.
  4. Send the labels. We publish the agreement statistic whatever it says.

Also wanted: a system that performs L1, a better proposer than the one that never offered sin(3x), or an attack on the boundary in the section above. If you can automate a theorem-grade internal constraint, that is the four-eighths this cannot reach.
