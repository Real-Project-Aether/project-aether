# Mechanism: how the ventral cord generates and propagates the undulatory wave

## The chain

1. **Command drive.** Tonic activity of the forward command interneuron AVB depolarises the
   B-class motor neurons through gap junctions and chemical synapses, setting them near threshold.

2. **Segmental oscillators.** Each B-class motor neuron and the D-class neuron it excites form a
   half-centre: the B neuron drives the dorsal (or ventral) muscle and, through D, inhibits the
   opposite side. With a slow potassium current, each segment oscillates on its own at 0.3-0.6 Hz.

3. **Coupling along the cord.** Neighbouring segments are coupled anteriorly to posteriorly by the
   B-class chemical synapses, with a delay of about 0.15 s per segment, so the oscillation of each
   segment lags the one in front: a travelling wave from head to tail.

4. **Wavelength and frequency.** The phase lag per segment sets the wavelength; raising the command
   drive raises the frequency of every segment without changing the lag, so the wave shortens in
   time but keeps its spatial pattern.

5. **A-class neurons.** The A class is quiet during forward movement and active in reversal; it
   does not take part in the forward wave.

## Evidence

Drive scans of AVB and segment isolation experiments (experiment.log). The isolated segment
oscillates at 0.42 Hz; coupled, the wave travels at 1.8 segments per second.

## Insights

The forward wave is generated in the cord itself and only started by the command interneuron;
the head is not needed for the wave to run.
